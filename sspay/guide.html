The hidden cost

Small fees. Every single day.

Most UK bank cards weren't built for living abroad. Three charges add up fast.

Card payments

A foreign transaction fee.

Many UK cards add around 2–4% to every payment in ringgit.1 It hides inside the exchange rate, so you rarely see it.

Cash

ATM charges, twice.

Your UK bank can charge a cash fee, and the Malaysian ATM can add its own on top.

At the till

"Pay in pounds?"

When a terminal offers to charge you in GBP, it usually sets its own rate. Always choose ringgit.

The setup we recommend

Four steps. One afternoon.

Keep your UK bank for UK bills. Use a multi-currency account for everything in Malaysia.

  1. Open a multi-currency account.

    Sign up through our partner link. You'll need your passport and a UK address or your Malaysian address.

  2. Move pounds across when you need them.

    Top up from your UK bank in GBP, then convert to MYR at the provider's rate with its fee shown upfront.

  3. Add the card to Apple Pay or Google Pay.

    Pay by phone across Malaysia. Keep the physical card for ATMs and places that don't take contactless.

  4. Always pay in ringgit.

    If a terminal or ATM asks whether to charge you in pounds, say no.

Questions

Good to know.

Is SSPay a bank or money changer?

No. SSPay is a guide and setup service. We don't hold money, exchange currency or issue cards. We point you to a licensed provider and help with the setup.

Do you earn anything from the link?

Yes. The provider may pay us a referral fee when you sign up and make your first transfer. It doesn't change your price.

Should I close my UK bank account?

Usually not. Keep it for your pension, salary, direct debits and UK bills. Use the multi-currency account for spending in Malaysia.

Is this financial advice?

No. This page is general information. Your situation may differ, so check the details with the provider or a qualified adviser.
